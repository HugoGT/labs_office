# Build the office one block at a time

New offices start as a black void with one central wood block, the entrance. Admins paint 9 × 9 blocks with a floor palette in the office sidebar and raise walls on the grid lines between tiles with a wall palette, lay out furniture (chairs anyone can sit on, desk tables, room tables, plants) from **Decoración**, then add rooms and desks on top. Painting changes **terrain and walls only**: it never deletes rooms, desks, decor, users, or saved collision settings.

## Quick path

1. Sign in as an admin or superadmin with the directory configured. Open the office sidebar's **Terreno** section.
2. Pick a floor in the palette (for example **Madera**). It stays highlighted and picked.
3. Click blocks on the map. Each click paints the 9 × 9 block under the pointer at once and for everyone. To paint an area, hold the button down and drag: every block the stroke crosses is painted, each once, until you release it. Painting over the void builds new terrain, painting over terrain replaces it.
4. To erase, pick **Vacío** and click blocks: they go back to void. Click the picked floor again, **Deseleccionar**, or press Escape to stop painting.
5. For barriers, pick a wall under **Paredes** (**Ladrillo**, **Piedra**, **Yeso** or **Vidrio**) and click or drag: each grid vertex (tile corner) nearest the pointer gets a wall post, joined to its 4-adjacent posts by a wall along the grid line, so a wall sits on the edge between two terrains, half on each. The cursor is a tile-sized box centered on that vertex. **Quitar pared** removes posts the same way. Only one entry of all palettes is picked at a time.
6. For furniture, open **Personalizar › Decoración** (its first entry for admins). Pick a piece under **Sillas**, **Mesas de escritorio**, **Mesas de sala** or **Plantas** (each tile names only what sets it apart in its group, "Madera" under **Sillas**, with the full name as its title; a room table also shows its size, like "7x5") and click a tile: a translucent ghost shows where it lands, outlined red where it does not fit (water, void, a wall, the entrance, base furniture, another piece). One click places one piece and the menu comes back; tick **Múltiple: varios a la vez** first to keep the piece picked and place several in a row until Escape. A colorable piece (**Pintado**, the painted desk table) has a color swatch under its tile: choose the color there (curated presets or **Otro color**) before picking it, since the menu steps aside while a piece is picked; the ghost shows that color. While a chair, a desk table or a room table is picked, the mouse wheel turns it instead of zooming (one notch, one turn; the wheel zooms again once nothing turnable is picked), and so does R on the map (Shift+R back): chairs and desk tables turn a quarter, room tables switch between horizontal and vertical. A piece placed on the anchor tile of another replaces, turns or repaints it. With nothing picked, click a placed piece to select it: it is outlined, with a four-arrows button and a red trash button under it. The arrows pick it up (turn it with the wheel or R if you like) and the next click puts it down there; the trash, Supr or Backspace delete it. Escape cancels a move (the piece stays selected), then the selection. Anyone can sit on a placed chair by walking into it, like the base chairs.
7. Desks and rooms live together in **Editar espacios**, one of the two editing the map at a time: it opens on the room form with two full-width buttons under **Escritorios**: **Editar escritorios** swaps the rooms for the desk list (**Volver** swaps them back) and **Crear escritorio** places a desk straight away, then returns to the rooms; with **Múltiple** ticked next to it, every placement (created or refused) starts the next one until Escape or **Cancelar**. A new desk asks nothing and is an empty 3 × 3 area where you click (no label, no material, no table or chair of its own); in the list, each desk is one line with its name, a **Mover** button and a red trash button that deletes it; a desk picked on the map still offers **Mover**, **Eliminar** and **Deseleccionar**. **Editar salas**, above the room form, does the same for rooms: it swaps the desks and the form for the room list, with the same rows (name, **Mover**, red trash) and **Volver**; a room picked on the map still offers **Mover**, **Eliminar** and **Deseleccionar**. Both lists take the whole panel height and scroll with it. Escape steps out one level per press: a selection or a placement first, then either list back to the default view, then the editor itself (back to the **Personalizar** list); the next press closes the panel. Desks are only claimable zones: creating or moving one over furniture is allowed and never removes it (only between two desk tables is it refused: a desk holds one), and a desk keeps its furniture when it changes hands. Whoever claims a desk and does not administer decorates it from **Personalizar › Decoración**, the same menu limited to that desk's 3 × 3 and to pieces that fit it (one piece per click, no **Múltiple**), and can give it back there with **Dejar escritorio**. A desk holds at most one desk table (placing another replaces it), three chairs and six people: a seventh cannot walk in.
8. Under **Salas** (shown when **Editar espacios** opens), the form starts filled in: the first free name "Sala N" (case-insensitive against the existing rooms, back to the next free one after each room created), one office block (9 × 9, sides under 6 tiles refused, #184) and a default capacity, one more than the longer side (it follows the size until typed; a typed capacity is kept as is, with no upper limit); overwrite any of them. Click **Colocar nueva sala** and click a built block. A 9 × 9 room snaps exactly to the block under the pointer; other room sizes keep tile placement. With **Múltiple** ticked in the form, each created room starts placing the next one with the same size and capacity and the next name ("Sala 1", "Sala 2", ...; a name without a number gets " 2"; names already taken are skipped), and a refused one is placed again; Escape or **Cancelar** stops and leaves the next room in the form. Room CRUD and overlap rules are unchanged.

## Map contract

| Topic | Contract |
|---|---|
| World | 189 × 135 tiles, 6048 × 4320 px; 21 × 15 blocks (`MAP_BLOCK_COLUMNS`, `MAP_BLOCK_ROWS` in `src/game/mapData.ts`) |
| Office block | 9 × 9 tiles, 288 × 288 px; all 81 tiles share the block's material |
| Spawn | Tile `(94, 67)`, the world's center, in central block index `157` (column 11, row 8 counting from 1); it stays wood: incompatible saved overrides are ignored at startup, and edits cannot change it |
| Void | Material `void`: no terrain. Nonwalkable exactly like water. Drawn as nothing over a black background (`VOID_COLOR`), on the map and the minimap, and outside the world too. Next to void every floor ends straight on the tile line: it runs on under a black void cap with a 1 px rim |
| Floors | Carpet, tile, wood, grass, cobblestone, dirt, sand and water, from the highest drawing priority down (where two meet, the higher one is drawn over the lower one's edge; the palette lists them in this order, then the void eraser); water is a normal floor and stays nonwalkable |
| Borders | No displaced/jittered block lookup. The art pack's dual-grid transitions and intentional half-tile rendering origin remain |
| Walls | One wall piece of the art pack per post (`WALL_PIECES`: `wall-brick`, `wall-stone`, `wall-plaster`, `wall-glass`); the entry of tile (tx, ty) stands on the grid vertex at its top-left corner. A wall blocks its footprint rectangles (`wallFootprintRects`: a 16 × 16 joint per post, a body between 4-adjacent posts) over any terrain or bridge, never whole tiles; frames come from the neighboring posts (`wallSprites`) |
| Decor | One map-level layer of art pieces anchored per tile (`src/game/officeDecor.ts`): chairs (1 × 1, a seat `chair-<tile>`, walk-through by default), desk tables (3 × 1 or 1 × 3, anchored on the middle tile, solid under their top; a colorable one in a color of its own), room tables (manifest footprint centered on the anchor, swapped when vertical, solid) and plants (walk-through). Chairs and desk tables turn 0, 90, 180, 270 (down, right, up, left), room tables 0 and 90 (horizontal, vertical); plants do not |
| Default | Void everywhere but the entrance; no ground overrides, decals, walls, hedges, props, decor, labels, or fallback rooms |

## Painting, conflicts and players

Each painted block (a click, or each block a drag crosses) is one `POST /admin/terrain/blocks/:index` with `{ material }`. The editor sends them one at a time, in stroke order, and draws each one as pending until the room replicates it; collisions never follow a pending paint. The atomic batch `POST /admin/terrain/blocks` (no sidebar control uses it) takes `{ edits: [{ index, material }], expected }`, where `expected` is the replicated block-list wire string the editor last saw. Both require admin/superadmin authorization and run in the same serialization queue. A batch validates all indices and materials, rejects duplicates and oversized requests, and writes all changed rows with a single atomic PostgreSQL upsert statement (or one synchronous memory mutation).

Water and void are refused under placements (stored rooms, desks, decor, protected static furniture, seats and the spawn area) with `terrain-under-placement`, never under players. A refused paint is dropped from the pending ones and its reason shown; the others go on. Lost authorization or configuration stops painting. A batch sent against a changed terrain is refused with `terrain-stale`; retry. Protection failures, stale batches and failed atomic writes leave **both terrain and player positions unchanged**. Missing directory/store keeps editing unavailable (503).

After persistence and publication of an accepted snapshot, the room relocates every avatar whose full physical footprint is no longer walkable to the safe primary spawn/ring, standing. Reserved reconnecting players move too; reconnect replays that position rather than resurrecting an invalid return cell. A replicated `positionRevision` distinguishes relocation from ordinary movement echoes: the client cancels held movement, auto-walk waypoints and pending double clicks, resets its complete Arcade body coordinates, and rechecks spaces/audio. Peers snap instead of tweening across removed ground. The server refuses move/sit messages from before that revision. Saved-position restoration also checks the full footprint against terrain and piece collisions.

## Walls

Walls are stored per grid vertex in `terrain_walls` (`tile_index`, `piece_id`, `updated_by`, `updated_at`), only where a post stands: `tile_index` is `ty * width + tx` and names the vertex at the top-left corner of tile (tx, ty). Removing a post deletes its row, and a stored post wins over the layout's on that vertex (the shipped layout has none). Rows stored before posts moved onto vertices were tile walls; they now read as posts on those tiles' top-left corners, half a tile up and left of where they were drawn, with no migration. `POST /admin/terrain/walls` takes `{ edits: [{ index, piece }] }`, `piece` a wall piece or `null` to remove it, at most `MAX_WALL_EDITS` (2000) distinct vertices; invalid input answers 400 `invalid-request`, a missing directory or store 503 `terrain-not-configured`. It runs in the same queue as block edits and writes the whole batch in one atomic statement (or one synchronous memory mutation).

A post is refused with `terrain-under-placement` when its footprint (the four tiles around its vertex, `wallPostTiles`) touches a desk (its 3 × 3 footprint), a decor piece, a base chair, protected static furniture or the spawn area. Rooms do not refuse walls: walls inside and around rooms are the point. Players never refuse a wall; the room relocates whoever's 18 × 14 body a wall rectangle now overlaps exactly like after a block edit. Stored posts whose footprint touches a protected static tile are ignored at startup without rewriting the row.

Desks refuse walls too: creating a desk, or moving one, whose 3 × 3 footprint a wall rectangle overlaps (positive area: a post on the footprint's edge line counts) answers 409 `desk-on-wall` ("Hay una pared en ese lugar..."). The check runs in the terrain edit queue, so a wall painted at the same moment lands either before it (and refuses the desk) or after it (and is refused on the desk). Renaming, claiming and releasing a desk never look at walls.

The live walls are part of the terrain snapshot (`terrainSnapshot(layout, blocks, walls)`, carrying `wallRects` and `wallTiles`), with the same pairing as piece collisions: the server refuses a move whose body center falls inside a wall rectangle (half open), the client collides its full Arcade body with static bodies for the same ground (`wallColliderRects`, merged into straight runs), and `grid.solid` (pathfinding, free-tile search, the e2e teleport) blocks every tile a wall rectangle touches at all, so the client is always the stricter side. Walls never take a tile from a room's floor. The room replicates them as `OfficeState.terrainWalls`, a sparse `<tile>:<material>` list (`encodeTerrainWalls`). The sidebar sends consecutive pending wall paints as one request, draws them as pending until the room replicates them, and never collides with a pending wall.

## Decor

Every piece of furniture is one row of `office_decor` (`tile_index`, the anchor tile `ty * width + tx`; `piece_id`; `rotation`; `color`, a strict lower-case `#rrggbb` or NULL, added by an `ALTER TABLE ... ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS` so existing databases migrate; `updated_by`, `updated_at`), only where one stands; removing one deletes its row. The layer is independent of desks and rooms: a desk is a claimable 3 × 3 zone, and what stands on it stays when the desk is freed, claimed by someone else, moved or deleted.

| Kind | Pieces | Footprint and anchor | Turns | Default collision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `chair` | `chair-*` | 1 × 1, the anchor tile | yes, its facing | none; a seat |
| `desk` | `desk-*` desk tables, pack and uploads | 3 × 1 along a row (0, 180) or 1 × 3 along a column (90, 270), the anchor is the middle tile | yes; a colorable one (`desk-painted`) also takes a `color` | the floor under its top |
| `table` | `table-*` room tables (`table-meeting` 7 × 5, `table-cafeteria` 5 × 3, `table-oval` 2 × 1) | from the manifest, centered on the anchor; vertical (90) the footprint swapped (7 × 5 becomes 5 × 7) | 0 horizontal and 90 vertical only (symmetric) | the whole footprint, turned with it |
| `plant` | `plant-*`, pack and uploads | from the manifest (1 × 1 for the pack), centered on the anchor | no | none |

`POST /admin/decor` (admin or superadmin, anywhere) and `POST /me/decor` (anyone signed in, only inside the desk they occupy) take `{ edits: [{ index, item }] }`, `item` a `{ piece, rotation, color? }` or `null` to remove the piece anchored on `index`, at most `MAX_DECOR_EDITS` (500) distinct anchors. `color` is a `#rrggbb` (any case, stored lower case; absent or `null` is none) for a colorable piece only; a colorable piece without one draws in its manifest default. Invalid input, a piece the catalog does not offer (unknown or retired: retired pieces already placed keep drawing), a turn the piece does not take (a plant any, a room table 180 or 270), a color on a piece that is not colorable or a malformed color answers 400 `invalid-request`; a missing directory or store 503 `terrain-not-configured`. `POST /me/decor` answers 403 `no-desk` without a claimed desk and 403 `outside-desk` when a piece it places, replaces or removes is not wholly inside that desk. Both run in the same queue as block and wall edits and write the whole batch atomically.

A placed piece is refused with `terrain-under-placement` when its footprint leaves the map or touches water, void, a tile a wall rectangle touches at all, a base chair, protected static furniture, the spawn area or another piece, judged on the whole layer after the batch. Desks, rooms and players never refuse decor, and decor never refuses a desk. Decor pieces are protected placements in turn: a block edit that would turn one of their tiles into water or void, and a wall post whose footprint touches one, answer `terrain-under-placement`. Stored rows that break today's rules (from before a rule, or copied from the old tables) are kept and drawn; only new edits are checked.

The room replicates the layer as `OfficeState.officeDecor`, a `<index>:<piece>:<rotation>[:<color>]` list (`encodeOfficeDecor`; the color only on a painted piece, so entries without one read as before). The scene draws a painted piece through the art pack's ramp recolor, one cached texture per piece and color, and a vertical room table in the second frame of its sheet. `sit { seat: 'chair-<tile>' }` seats anyone within one tile of a decor chair that stands there and nobody occupies, facing the chair's way; turning it turns its sitter, removing or replacing it stands them up. Each piece is a collision instance of its own piece id (`officeDecorInstances`), so a collision edit of `chair-*`, `desk-*`, `table-*` or `plant-*` applies to the decor and the layout alike.

The editor (**Decoración**: the whole map for an admin, one's desk for anyone else) sends consecutive pending clicks as one request, one at a time, draws them as pending until the room replicates them, and drops only a refused piece with its reason; **Múltiple** stays on through a refusal. While a piece is picked or one is selected the map is held (no walking or dragging); only a picked piece also makes the right rail and the minimap step aside. Escape puts the piece down first (a cancelled move leaves it selected), then deselects, then leaves **Decoración**. Limited to a desk, only pieces wholly inside it can be selected.

The old furniture moved into the layer once, on the first start of this code, behind the one-row `office_decor_version` marker: every chair of the former `terrain_chairs` (its facing turned into a rotation) and the art decor of every desk occupant's former per-person desk configuration (`user_desk_configs`), on the tile of its slot box. Decor of people without a desk and legacy bundle sprites were dropped. Both old tables stay in the schema, read only by that copy; a restart never adds back a piece an admin removed afterwards.

Existing placements are never removed to make an edit succeed. Move or remove them separately through their normal authorized tools. Terrain edits do not carve paths automatically through stored rooms or collisions.

## Upgrading from the 14 × 10 grid

The first block editor stored rows against a 14 × 10 block grid (126 × 90 tiles). That grid now sits at block offset (+3 columns, +2 rows) of the new one, so the old spawn block 77 is the new central block 157 and everything keeps its place around it.

`server/src/directory/schema.sql` moves stored rows **exactly once**, on the first start of the new code:

| Rows | Move |
|---|---|
| `terrain_blocks` | index `r * 14 + c` becomes `(r + 2) * 21 + (c + 3)`; rows past the old grid (index ≥ 140, never read) are deleted |
| `spaces`, `desks` | `x + 27`, `y + 18` (they store tiles); decor in `space_layouts` is relative to its space |
| `users.last_x`, `last_y` | `+ 864`, `+ 576` px when saved; `NULL` stays `NULL` |

The move runs only while the one-row `map_layout_version` table is empty, and writes version `2` (`MAP_LAYOUT_VERSION`) in the same implicit transaction as the whole schema script: a crash moves nothing, and a restart never moves anything twice. Rows go through a free index/coordinate range first, so no primary key or exclusion constraint sees a moved row on top of one still waiting. A new database runs it over empty tables and just gets the marker. Blocks explicitly saved as water stay water; blocks nobody saved had no row and are now void.

Rollback: reverting the code does not move rows back. Older code would read the moved rows against the 14 × 10 grid, so restore from a backup taken before the upgrade (daily backups and point-in-time recovery, see `infra/gcp/README.md`) if a rollback must also restore data.

## Reproduction

`pnpm map:init` reproduces the checked-in void `src/game/maps/office.json` and the Tiled palette (`void` is its last tile, solid black). It writes repository artifacts only, not database rows. `--palette-only` preserves manual Tiled edits. Keep the default's per-tile overlay layers empty if runtime block edits must control every tile.

Tests use explicit legacy fixtures (`src/test/legacyLayout.ts`, its own 126 × 90 world) for base chairs, furniture, rooms, collision and proximity behavior. Real-process E2E starts `e2e/server.ts` with an isolated in-memory topology; it does not seed a live database or reinstate legacy objects in the shipped default.
